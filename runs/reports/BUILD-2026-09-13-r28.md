# BUILD 2026-09-13-r28 — P0: the dungeon was unplayable (lighting), and a gate for legibility

- **Date:** 2026-09-14 01:42 EDT
- **Owner:** forge
- **Status:** PASS
- **Trigger:** the owner looked at a screenshot of floor 1 and reported: *"map is dark, large
  pixelated circles, it didnt even look like a map or playable map - no sprites, no monster sprites,
  no props, etc."* — a P0 by the standing rule that a crash **or an unplayable view** outranks features.

---

## 1. What was actually wrong — three defects, all in `game/engine/lighting.py`

Reproduced first, from a real frame through the real render path
(`--headless --seed 0 --shot 120`): the frame measured **73.7% of pixels below 16/255 luminance**,
**33.2% of 32 px tiles visible**, and vision reported *"5 large, rounded, soft-edged shapes with
wavy/scalloped outer borders"* in orange/yellow/purple — the owner's "large pixelated circles".

| # | Defect | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The "fog" step multiplied the whole frame by the ambient colour `(11, 10, 16)` under `BLEND_RGB_MULT` | Every pixel — floor included — was reduced to ~4% brightness. The catacombs tile art measures **78 mean luminance**; multiplied by 4% it renders at ~26, i.e. black. The scene was dark, not lit. |
| 2 | Each tile inside a light stamped a **solid circle of radius `TILE`** (32 px → a 64 px disc) | A 140 px lantern stamped ~190 overlapping solid discs. That IS the "large pixelated circles": no gradient at all, with tiered edges where the per-tile alpha quantised. |
| 3 | The line-of-sight mask is indexed by **level** tile but was queried with **screen** tile indices | Shadows landed in arbitrary places, unrelated to the player or the walls. |

## 2. The repair

`render_lighting` now builds a proper **light map** and multiplies the scene by it once:

1. fill the map at a per-biome ambient level (near-white: multiplication can only darken);
2. additively blit a **cached radial gradient** per light (`(1 - t)²` falloff baked into the **RGB**
   values, not the alpha — see §4);
3. darken tiles the player cannot see, mapped in **level** coordinates
   (`world_tile = (screen_px + offset) // TILE`);
4. `BLEND_RGB_MULT` the scene by the finished map.

The visibility sweep is also memoised per (level, player tile) — it is ~900 Bresenham rays and it was
being recomputed every frame.

## 3. Measured, before → after (same seed, same tick, same render path)

| Metric | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Visible 32 px tiles | 33.2% | **100.0%** |
| Mid-tone pixels (16-80) | 4.5% | **51.2%** |
| Near-black pixels (<16) | 73.7% | **28.5%** |
| Distinct colours | 806 | **5,449** |
| Light gradient (distinct luminance levels on a scanline) | 12.7 avg / 18 max | **33.6 avg / 51 max** |
| Frame cost | — | **3.23 ms (309 fps equivalent)**, game reports `fps_equiv=233` |

**Entities are drawn — proven, not assumed.** With the palette locked to 26 colours, "sprite colours
are present" proves nothing, so the player was isolated by rendering the same frame with the player
hidden and diffing:

```
player rect 32x32 at screen (80,80)
  pixels changed when the player is hidden : 250 / 1024 (24%)
  -> sprite IS drawn
```

and every entity sampled at *its own world position*: `wax_wisp` monsters 22-71 distinct colours each,
`prop_brazier` 46 colours at 110 luminance, `prop_sarcophagus` 32 at 168, `prop_altar` 43 at 159. They
were always being drawn — the darkness was crushing them to invisibility.

## 4. A bug in my own first fix, caught by measuring

I initially carried the gradient in the **alpha** channel. `BLEND_RGB_ADD` adds raw RGB and **ignores
alpha**, so the whole disc added at full strength — a flat blob, and the lantern left the player's own
tile at `1 distinct colour`. Baking the falloff into the RGB values fixed it. The probe that caught it
samples the pixel block at the player's own position; "the lantern doesn't light the lantern-holder"
is exactly the kind of thing that looks fine in a screenshot and is obviously broken on screen.

## 5. Why no gate caught this — and the new gate

P4.1 shipped with an acceptance metric of **"centre/brightness ratio > 4.0 across seeds"**. That is a
*contrast* measurement, and it passed happily on a frame that was 74% near-black with giant orange
discs on it. Contrast was excellent. Nothing was visible. A blank-frame check (`>2 KB, >8 colours`)
passes too — the broken frame had 806 colours.

New gate: **`tools.qa.scene_legibility`** (stdlib + pygame + numpy, no torch), wired into
`tools.selftest` (18 → 19 checks). It renders a real frame and asserts four things a broken view
fails, with thresholds taken from the broken frame's own measurements:

| Check | Threshold | Broken frame |
|---|---|---|
| visible-tile coverage | ≥ 85% | 33.2% ✗ |
| mid-tone share | ≥ 25% | 4.5% ✗ |
| near-black share | ≤ 45% | 73.7% ✗ |
| distinct colours | ≥ 500 | 806 ✓ (necessary, not sufficient) |

**Proven to fail before being trusted** — it is run against the actual broken frame, which is still on
disk:

```
$ python -m tools.qa.scene_legibility --frame %TEMP%/rw_look_floor1.png
  visible tiles          33.2%  (want >= 85%)
  mid-tone share          4.5%  (want >= 25%)
  near-black share       73.7%  (want <= 45%)
  [FAIL] only 33% of tiles are visible (need >= 85%) - the map is dark
  [FAIL] only 4.5% of pixels are mid-tone (need >= 25%) - no readable detail
  [FAIL] 73.7% of pixels are near-black (limit 45%)
FAILED: the floor is not readable            (exit 1)

$ python -m tools.qa.scene_legibility
OK: the floor is legible                      (exit 0)
```

`--frame PATH` also lets lens audit any saved PNG without re-running the game.
