# BUILD 2026-09-14 — r39: the studio org chart (authority, reporting lines, tiers)

Date: 2026-09-14.  Owner: forge.  Lane: docs/ + every bot's SOUL.md.

## Why

The studio was **flat**: five lanes, a reviewer and a music bot, with no authority chain, no
reporting lines and no escalation path. The owner asked whether the bots were set up in an
org-chart sense and supplied a corporate template (~29 roles). They were not.

## What changed

- **New `docs/ORG.md`** — the authority structure: the chain, who decides what, the escalation
  path, and an explicit list of template roles this studio does **not** staff and why.
- **Every bot's `SOUL.md`** gained a *Your place in the studio* block carrying its own row of the
  chart (reports to / receives from / decides / escalates to). Seven profiles updated. A reporting
  line that disagrees with the SOULs is worse than no chart, because bots only act on what loads in
  their own context.
- **`docs/STUDIO.md`** now points at `docs/ORG.md`.

## The structure

```
OWNER
  |-- FORGE  (director)  CEO / Studio Director / Game Director - merged, one game
  |     |-- CHIP   Technical Director   game/, tools/
  |     |-- PIXEL  Art Director         assets/, tools/art/
  |     |-- LORE   Design & Content     game/data/
  |     |-- TEMPO  Audio Director       assets/audio/
  |     `-- LENS   QA / Playtest        runs/, tools/qa/
  `-- WARDEN (editor)  Standards & QA Director - reports to the OWNER, not forge
```

## The two judgment calls (both departures from the template)

1. **WARDEN reports to the Owner, not to forge.** The template puts QA under the COO/Studio
   Director. Here forge both assigns the work and integrates it, so a warden under forge would be
   reviewing its own director's output. Independence is the whole point of the seat.
2. **The IC ladder is a model ladder.** The template's Senior/Mid/Junior/Intern rows are not four
   more bots; they are tiers *within* a lane, and which tier may do which work is measured
   (`docs/MODELS.md`): fast local models are interns fit only for isolated generation (they destroy
   any file they edit in place), free-tier cloud executors are the senior specialists, local bulk
   generation is the mid tier, and WARDEN is the principal reviewer. Measured on identical work, the
   same cloud models failed an open-ended task and passed a precise directive — so **the brief-writer
   is the load-bearing role, not the model tier.**

## Not staffed, on purpose

No CFO (no money is spent - the real constraint is token/rate-limit discipline, which is law 5 and
WARDEN's job), no HR (no humans are hired; the analogue - model onboarding - is forge's, §5 of the
chart), no CMO/marketing/community (the game is not sold or announced), no associate producers
(seven agents report in writing, so there is no coordination cost to manage).

Test recorded for any future seat: **does it have a distinct acceptance command and an artifact only
it can produce?** If not, it generates meetings instead of artifacts.
